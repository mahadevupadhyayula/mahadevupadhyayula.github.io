### Business objective

Reduce manual evidence reconciliation and improve discovery preparation by giving the accountable reviewer a complete, traceable Product Evidence Pack. The goal is not to automate roadmap prioritization.

### What the workflow produces

A **Product Evidence Pack** showing:

- A normalized customer problem statement, affected users, operating conditions, and current workarounds.
- Included and excluded evidence with reviewer-visible rationale.
- Account-aware evidence clusters that avoid duplicate-account inflation.
- Relevant support, implementation, usage, commercial, research, and win/loss context.
- Supporting and contradictory evidence, evidence gaps, confidence limits, and unanswered research questions.
- Field-level source links for every material factual claim.
- A proposed next discovery action—not a roadmap priority.

### Human approval boundary

A product manager or product-operations analyst reviews the evidence pack and its citations, edits the normalized problem statement, includes or excludes records, splits or merges proposed clusters, corrects segment interpretation, and chooses the next discovery action.

Only after that decision does the workflow prepare a simulated product-discovery record, linked evidence set, research tasks, or portfolio-review summary. It does **not** prioritize the roadmap, estimate market size, contact customers, create product requirements, or claim that a requested capability will produce revenue.

### Representative prototype scope

**In scope:** Synthetic data for one defined request theme; 20–30 feature requests, support cases, CRM notes, customer-success context, usage summaries, research, win/loss evidence, current product documentation, and a known workaround; duplicate records, conflicting evidence, distinct needs, and missing context; evidence-linked normalization; reviewer editing; and simulated discovery output.

**Not in scope:** Live enterprise integrations, autonomous roadmap prioritization, sentiment scoring, market or revenue forecasting, customer segmentation decisions, competitive intelligence, product-requirement generation, customer outreach, or production deployment controls.

### What a pilot should prove

1. Material claims in the evidence pack have valid field-level citations.
2. Duplicate records from the same account do not inflate apparent demand.
3. Reviewers agree that included evidence supports the normalized problem statement.
4. Contradictory evidence and important gaps remain visible.
5. Reviewers can correct segment interpretation and choose a justified next discovery action.
6. Preparation time and material reviewer rewriting decrease without unsupported strategic conclusions.
